Keeping your binder secure: where to store it and who gets access

Safe enough to protect it. Easy enough for family to find.

A binder full of account details needs some protection. But a binder no one can reach doesn't help either. Most families look for a middle ground.

Common places people keep it

PlaceUpsideWatch out for
Fireproof home safeProtected, easy for family to reachSomeone needs the code or key
Locked file drawerSimple and cheapNot fire- or flood-proof
Bank safe deposit boxVery secureFamily may not be able to open it quickly after a death; rules vary by state and bank
With a trusted relativeOff-site backupThey need to keep it safe too

Who usually knows

Many people tell one or two people where the binder is and how to get to it: often the executor, a spouse, or an adult child. Telling them now saves them a search later.

Digital copies

If the templates are kept in Google Drive, the Drive account becomes part of the plan. Many people share the folder with one trusted person, or make sure that person can get into the account through Google's Inactive Account Manager.

Good to know

A short note on the first page of the binder, saying where the originals are kept, helps whoever opens it.
