After a loss

Who pays for the funeral, and can the estate pay you back?

Whoever signs the contract is on the hook, at least at first.

Current as of October 2026. Laws and figures can change — see full disclaimer.

The person who signs the funeral home contract is usually responsible for paying it. If the person who died left money, the estate (everything they owned) can often pay that person back.

How it usually works

  • Funeral costs are generally among the first debts an estate pays, ahead of most others. Exact rules vary by state.
  • Families who pay first usually keep every receipt so the executor can reimburse them.
  • If the estate doesn't have enough money, the person who signed may be left paying.

Where the money often comes from

SourceNotes
Life insurancePaid to the named beneficiary, often within weeks
Payable-on-death accountsPaid directly to the named beneficiary with a death certificate
A prepaid funeral planCheck what it covers
Social SecurityA one-time $255 payment to a surviving spouse or child who qualifies. It isn't paid to the funeral home.
Veterans benefitsBurial allowances for eligible veterans
Employer or unionSome offer a death benefit or life insurance
Workers' compensationIf the death was work-related
State or county assistanceSome offer help with basic burial for families who can't pay

FEMA sometimes helps with funeral costs after a federally declared disaster.

Good to know

Some funeral homes will wait for a life insurance payout if the family signs an assignment form, which lets the insurer pay part of the payout straight to the funeral home. It's worth asking.
