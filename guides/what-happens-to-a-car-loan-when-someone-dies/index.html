What happens to a car loan when someone dies

The car and the loan go together.

When someone dies with a car loan, the loan becomes a debt of their estate. The lender still holds the title until it's paid off, so the car can't be sold or transferred without dealing with the loan.

The usual options

  • Keeping up payments. Whoever inherits the car can often keep making the payments. Some lenders let them take over the loan, and others ask them to refinance in their own name.
  • Paying it off. The estate pays the balance, often from savings or life insurance, and the title is released.
  • Selling the car. The sale pays off the loan, and anything left goes to the estate.
  • Returning it. If no one wants the car, the lender can take it back. If it sells for less than is owed, the estate may still owe the difference.

If there's a co-signer, they're usually responsible for the payments.

Leased cars

A lease is a rental contract, not a loan. Some leasing companies end the lease early after a death, and others expect payments to continue. The lease agreement says which.

Good to know

Some car loans come with credit life insurance, which can pay off part or all of the loan after a death. It's often listed in the loan paperwork. GAP coverage is different: it pays the shortfall if the car is totaled or stolen, not because the borrower died.
