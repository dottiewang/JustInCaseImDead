After a loss

It's okay to choose the simplest option

A simple funeral isn't a measure of love. Many families choose it on purpose.

Current as of October 2026. Laws and figures can change — see full disclaimer.

Families often feel that spending less on a funeral says something about how much they cared. It doesn't. Many people ask for the simplest option themselves, and many families choose it so they can put their time and money into a memorial that feels more personal.

The range, from simplest to most involved

OptionWhat it usually includesCost
Direct cremationTransfer, cremation, a basic container, ashes returned. No viewing beforehand.Lowest. Often a fraction of a full funeral, but prices vary widely by area.
Direct burialTransfer and burial soon after death, no viewing or embalmingLow, plus cemetery costs
Cremation with a funeral or viewing firstEmbalming, viewing or service, then cremationMedian $6,280 (NFDA, 2023)
Traditional funeral with burialEmbalming, viewing, service, hearse, casketMedian $8,300 (NFDA, 2023), plus cemetery costs

The medians come from the National Funeral Directors Association and don't include cemetery charges such as the plot, opening the grave or a headstone. Prices for the same service can differ by thousands of dollars between funeral homes in the same town.

What a simple option doesn't rule out

  • A memorial service later, at home, at a place of worship, in a park or anywhere that means something
  • Scattering or burying the ashes on a meaningful date
  • A gathering of any size, with or without a funeral home

What families often check in a quote

  • The all-in price, including the basic services fee (the funeral home's flat charge for staff and overhead), permits, the death certificate and any medical examiner fee
  • Whether a package includes things they didn't ask for, which can usually be removed
  • Whether the lowest-priced option on the itemized price list matches what was quoted on the phone

Phrases worth noticing

  • "What they deserve" or "what you'd want for them"
  • "Most families choose…" when describing a more expensive package
  • "This is required" without saying which law requires it

Under the FTC's Funeral Rule, funeral homes have to give an itemized price list, and families can choose only the goods and services they want, with some exceptions such as the basic services fee.

Good to know

Writing down a preference ahead of time, even one line like "Keep it simple, direct cremation is fine," takes the guilt out of the decision for family.

  • Median funeral with viewing and burial (2023, excl. cemetery): $8,300
